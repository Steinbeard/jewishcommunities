# The Nagid

## 1. What Daniel asked for

- **The lord of certain realms chooses one Jewish community leader to serve as Nagid.** *Daniel.*
- **The Nagid can serve the lord as a councillor or in a court position** such as physician,
  after Hasdai ibn Shaprut, Samuel ha-Nagid, Maimonides and others. *Daniel.*
- **Eligibility is gated on a host culture or a dynamic tenet/doctrine**, so that the office stays
  historical but can spread or vanish in play. *Daniel* (idea; §3 recommends the form).
- **The community must have high standing**, so the office caps a well-off, respected community. *Daniel.*

He also asked whether there were Ashkenazi Nagidim. §2 answers this.

## 2. Historical basis

Dates are for the writer. Verify them before citing in-game.

**Where the title was used.** *Nagid* ("leader, prince") was the Hebrew title of the recognised head
of the Jews of a Muslim realm. It is attested in Kairouan, Muslim Spain, Egypt and Yemen. In Egypt
the government post was *ra'is al-yahud*, "head of the Jews", and the two titles merged.

| Who | Where, when | Office at court | How he got the title |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hasdai ibn Shaprut | Córdoba, c. 940–970 | Physician to Abd al-Rahman III; diplomat (Byzantium, Queen Toda and Sancho of León) | Called *ha-Nasi*. He did the Nagid's work without the title |
| Abraham ibn Ata | Kairouan, early 11th c. | Physician to the Zirids Badis and al-Mu'izz | An honorific from Hai Gaon, 1015 (*negid ha-golah*) |
| Samuel ibn Naghrillah | Granada, 1027–1056 | Vizier of Habbus and Badis; commanded Granada's army | Title conferred 1027 |
| Joseph ibn Naghrillah | Granada, 1056–1066 | Vizier of Badis | Succeeded his father in both offices; killed 30 Dec 1066 |
| Judah b. Saadya, Mevorakh b. Saadya | Fatimid Egypt, from c. 1065 | Court physicians | Government office of head of the Jews (Cohen) |
| Maimonides; Abraham Maimonides | Ayyubid Egypt | Maimonides a physician in Saladin's circle; Abraham Nagid from 1205 | Maimonides' formal headship is debated. His descendants held the office for generations |
| Madmun b. Hasan | Aden, d. 1151 | Merchant representative | Nagid of Yemen |

The pattern is that a man rose at court on personal skill (medicine, Arabic letters, finance,
diplomacy) and his court office made him the realm's Jewish head. The court came first.

**Were there Ashkenazi Nagidim? No.** The title was never used in Ashkenaz. The communities of the
Rhineland, France and England governed themselves through local leaders, ordinances and synods.
They had no single head for a realm. Christian rulers did, however, create crown-appointed heads of
"their" Jews. These are the Ashkenazi-world analogues, and the communities resisted them:
- **England, the *Presbyter Judaeorum*, 1199–1290.** A crown appointee tied to the Exchequer of the
  Jews. Holders included Jacob of London (1199), Aaron of York and Elias le Evesque. The last was
  Hagin fil Deulacres, appointed in 1281.
- **The Empire, the *Hochmeister*, 1407.** King Rupert named a chief rabbi of the German Jews,
  identified with Israel of Krems. The purpose was mainly fiscal. Communities threatened him with
  excommunication. Rupert's second decree fined disobedience, but the office soon lapsed.
- **Christian Iberia.** Kings had Jewish courtiers before 1215, among them Cidellus (Joseph ibn
  Ferrizuel), physician to Alfonso VI. From the mid-13th century the crown appointed a
  ***Rab de la Corte*** in Castile and an ***Arrabi Mor*** in Portugal. These men divided taxes among
  the communities and supervised their leadership. Most were physicians, interpreters or fiscal
  agents, and few were noted scholars.

**What stopped it.** The obstacle was the host's law and teaching, not the Jews' culture:
- Byzantine Roman law barred Jews from office.
- Fourth Lateran (1215), canon 69, did the same in the Latin West. This is V31's Separation and
  Mission step.
- al-Mutawakkil (c. 850) and al-Hakim (c. 1009–1013) dismissed dhimmi officials. This is V31's
  Strict Pact step.
- The Almohads abolished dhimma altogether.

Ashkenazi Jews who moved to Toledo or Fustat could rise like anyone else.

## 3. Recommendation on the gate

**Use the host's teaching as the hard gate, Standing as the community gate, and culture for flavour
and AI weight only.**

1. **The dynamic doctrine Daniel asked for already exists.** V31's Teaching on the Jews is a doctrine
   on every Christian and Muslim rite. It is built and live-tested. It changes through councils and
   reform (V34), and its steps match the historical record in §2:

   | Teaching | Office allowed | Seats the Nagid may hold (§5) | Historical anchor |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Dhimma | **Yes** | Vizier (Chancellor), Steward, Marshal, Physician | Hasdai, Samuel, the Egyptian heads |
   | Strict Pact | No; a serving Nagid faces dismissal (§7.3) | none | al-Mutawakkil, al-Hakim |
   | Abolition | No; dismissed | none | the Almohads |
   | Witness | **Yes** | Chancellor, Steward, Physician | Cidellus; the Presbyter |
   | Separation and Mission | No; dismissal (§7.3) | none | Lateran IV c. 69 |
   | Exclusion | No; dismissed | none | |
   | Imperial Law | No | none | Theodosian and Justinian codes |
   | None (neutral fallback) | **Yes** | Chancellor, Steward, Physician | |

   Teachings change in play, so the office can spread, vanish and return. A Latin council that adopts
   Separation and Mission ends the Rab de la Corte. A Muslim ruler who drifts to Strict Pact dismisses
   his Nagid. One implementation, `kehillah_nagid_teaching_permits_trigger`, can read the existing
   teaching triggers.
2. **The host's Jewish Settlement law must be Allowed or Encouraged.** A lord who discourages or bans
   settlement does not appoint a head of the Jews.
3. **The community must have high Standing** (*Daniel*):
   - **To be named:** Standing (`kehillah_standing_value`) at least the Flourishing threshold (700).
   - **To keep the office:** at least Healthy (400). The gap stops a bad year from costing the office.

   Both are script values, `kehillah_nagid_appoint_standing_value` and
   `kehillah_nagid_retain_standing_value`. The office caps a community's growth, a goal the player can
   see coming.
4. **The leader must be personally qualified.** An adult who is free, Jewish and holds no other
   Nagid office, with at least one courtly skill (diplomacy, stewardship or learning) of 12 or more
   (tunable). The host's opinion must be at least 0.
5. **No hard culture lock, by default.** A culture lock would fix 1066 geography in place, which is
   the opposite of "dynamic". Culture instead does three things:
   - it weights the AI host's wish to appoint: strongly for Muslim and Iberian-heritage courts, weakly
     elsewhere (§4);
   - it names the office (§6);
   - the communities' rite sets how they receive it (§5.3).

   A Witness-teaching German emperor *can* name a "Hochmeister", but an AI emperor rarely will, and
   when he does, the Ashkenazi communities resent it. That is the historical texture.
6. **Optional game rule** for players who want the strict version: *Nagid office: Dynamic* (default,
   the gates above), *Historical* (adds a host heritage gate: Arabic, Berber, Iranian, Iberian and
   other MENA heritages only), or *Off*.

## 4. Choosing the Nagid

- **One Nagid per independent realm.** The realm of a community is its county holder's top liege,
  the same rule as `kehillah_is_host_of_community_trigger`. The office belongs to that ruler, even when
  a vassal holds the community's charter. It is personal, not hereditary.
- **The host's decision, "Name a Nagid."** Shown when the gates in §3.1–3.2 pass and the office is
  vacant. It opens an event with up to four eligible leaders in the realm, ranked by
  `kehillah_nagid_candidate_score_value`, and an option to leave the office vacant. The score
  weighs:
  - the best courtly skill;
  - the community's Standing;
  - the host's opinion;
  - physician or scholar traits;
  - **continuity:** a candidate of the previous Nagid's house scores higher. This lets the
    Naghrillah and Maimonidean patterns re-emerge without being scripted.
- **The community's petition, "Seek the Office of Nagid."** This is the main player path, since a
  player usually leads the community rather than the host. The petition goes to the host, through
  the Shtadlan if one is seated. The host's AI answers on the same score, with its outlook on the Jews
  (`kehillah_outlook_on_jews_value`) and greed added. If another community holds the office, the
  petition becomes **"Supplant the Nagid"** (slice 4). Communities in a realm then compete for the
  office.
- **The AI host** checks once a year from an on_action pulse. The chance to appoint is weighted by
  heritage, outlook band, greed and learning. Muslim and Iberian hosts with a Flourishing community
  should usually have a Nagid. Others should rarely appoint one.
- **Vacancy.** The office ends when:
  - the Nagid dies, steps down, converts or is imprisoned;
  - the community leaves the realm;
  - the teaching or the law gate fails (§7.3);
  - Standing falls below the retain threshold.

  The office never passes automatically to the community's next leader. The host is asked (§7.2).

## 5. What the office does

### 5.1 Service at court: the core ask

The Nagid can be appointed to the seats in §3.1's table: Chancellor (named "Vizier" for a Muslim
host), Steward, Marshal (Dhimma only, after Samuel), and Court Physician. **Whether the engine
allows this is the open risk; §9 decides it.**

- **Route A, preferred: a real seat.** The Nagid holds a vanilla council seat or court position of
  the host, with the host's council UI, council tasks and the position's modifiers.
  - A mod court position, `kehillah_nagid_court_position`, would make the office itself visible on
    the host's court screen.
  - For a Muslim host with a vizierate diarchy, the Nagid as diarch is the closest model of
    Samuel's office. This is a stretch goal.
- **Route B, fallback: service in name.** If the engine refuses, the host chooses a service, Vizier,
  Treasurer, Physician or Envoy. The host then gets a modifier scaled by the Nagid's matching skill,
  the way council modifiers scale. This loses the council UI and tasks, but keeps the choice and the
  pay-off.
- **Rejected:** making the leader the host's vassal. V32 records why: landless vassals need an
  administrative liege, and the charter and appointment succession rest on independence. Also
  rejected: moving the leader into the host's court, which would end their rule of the community.

### 5.2 For the Nagid's own community

- **Greatness and Stability baseline contributors** while the office is held, in the breakdown like
  every other contributor. V20 keeps one source of truth, so these are not one-off grants.
- **Protection:**
  - a Nagid term in the lord's protection during violence (`kehillah_host_protection_mult_value`,
    V36 §1);
  - the intercession answer in V40 crises and in the lord's demands.
- **Monthly prestige and influence** for the Nagid.
- **The nickname "ha-Nagid"** (`nick_kehillah_ha_nagid`, which already exists) after ten years in the
  Nagid-named form of the office.

### 5.3 For the other communities of the realm

The Nagid was head of all the realm's Jews:
- **"Under the Nagid's protection":** a small protection term for every other community in the realm.
- **Intercession** (slice 4). The Nagid is offered the intercession answer in another community's
  accusation or demand crisis. A player Nagid then plays the realm's crises, not only their own.
- **Reception by rite.** Communities of Minhag Ashkenaz (V45) also take a small Stability penalty,
  "a head set over us by the crown", unless the Nagid is from their own community. This follows the
  1407 resistance. Other rites take only the protection.

### 5.4 For the host

- **A capable servant** (§5.1).
- **The fiscal agent.** The Rab de la Corte divided taxes, and the Hochmeister was created for revenue.
  The realm's communities pay a slightly higher V31 §8 tribute while a Nagid serves. This multiplies
  the tribute value as built in
  [the host tribute](../implementation/2026-10-30-host-tribute.md).
- **Outlook:** a small positive term in the host's outlook on the Jews while the Nagid is in favour.

## 6. Names

The mechanics are the same everywhere. Customizable localization names the office by host and community:

| Host | Office name |
|---|---|
| Muslim faith, or neutral fallback | **Nagid** (tooltip: *ra'is al-yahud*, "head of the Jews") |
| Latin, Iberian heritage | **Rab de la Corte**; **Arrabi Mor** for a Portuguese-culture host |
| Latin, English culture or holder of England | **Presbyter of the Jews** |
| Latin, German-heritage culture | **Hochmeister** |
| Latin, elsewhere | **Master of the Jews** |

## 7. The cost: envy, the dilemma and the fall

The Nagid is powerful, so the office is dangerous. Joseph's death in 1066 is the anchor. Abu Ishaq's
poem attacks exactly this: a Jew with authority over Muslims.

### 7.1 Envy at court

While a Nagid holds a seat with authority (Vizier, Steward, Marshal), a **Nagid's envy** term grows
with tenure, up to a cap:
- it is added to the danger of **every community in the realm**, as a new component of
  `kehillah_danger_value`, beside the existing greatness and wealth terms;
- it gives V31 §7's anti-Jewish vassal faction a grievance.

A physician-only Nagid draws much less envy. The player chooses between influence and safety.

### 7.2 The Nagid's dilemmas, and successions

**Dilemmas.** Periodic events in which the host's interest and the community's pull apart: an
extraordinary levy on the realm's Jews, a loan on bad terms, or acting against a rival courtier.
Each answer trades the host's opinion against Stability.

**When the host dies**, the heir must confirm or dismiss the Nagid within six months. The AI weighs
outlook and opinion. Hasdai, for example, continued under al-Hakam II.

**When the Nagid dies or steps down**, the host is asked to name the new community leader, another
community's leader, or nobody.

### 7.3 Dismissal and the fall

**Dismissal.** If the teaching hardens past the gate, or the Settlement law drops to Discouraged, the
host gets an event:
- **dismiss**, the default for a zealous ruler;
- or **keep him quietly**, for a protective ruler. This costs piety and the clergy's opinion, and the
  office ends at the next succession.

This follows the Settlement law's rule that a harder policy never silently revokes an existing right.
V40 §5.3's "dismissal of dhimmis from office" decree, when built, calls the same dismissal effect.

**The fall.** A fall comes from:
- the host's opinion falling to −40 or below;
- a rival's scheme;
- a crisis that goes badly.

A fall dismisses the Nagid and may also:
- confiscate gold;
- open V32's arrest;
- add a **"the Nagid has fallen"** danger spike, lasting several years, to every community in the
  realm.

The fall of one man falls on all the Jews.

## 8. Starts and existing content

- **1066:** Joseph ibn Naghrillah (`d_kehillah_granada`) starts as Nagid of Badis's realm.
  `on_game_start` sets the office whatever his Standing, and the gates apply from the first
  reconfirmation. The authored Granada chain is unchanged. If it ends with his death, the office
  falls vacant, and Lucena or another community can later win it in play.
- **Egypt 1066:** the office is only just beginning (Cohen: "cautious beginnings, to 1082"). Leave it
  vacant for the AI to fill, unless research names the 1066 holder.
- **1178:** V51 decided that Maimonides is probably not the formal head in 1178. Do not seed him as
  Nagid. He can win the office by petition, as his son Abraham historically held it.
- **867:** none; the title is later.
- **Not in scope:** the Gaon's honorific, the route by which Hai Gaon named Ibn Ata. It belongs to
  [v47](v47-gaon-and-the-seat-of-the-sanhedrin.md)'s Gaon, once that is built (§12).

## 9. Slice 0: the engine spike (do this first)

Vanilla fills council seats from courtiers and vassals (`valid_character` reads
`root.liege_or_court_owner`). Court positions are scored "per eligible courtier/vassal"
(`_court_positions.info`). A Kehillah leader is an independent landless ruler with
`cannot_be_vassal_or_liege`, so neither pool contains them.

The spike tests whether the effects bypass the pool. Through `ck3_script`, in a 1066 debug game:
1. As Badis, run `assign_councillor_type = { type = councillor_chancellor target = <Granada leader>
   remove_existing_councillor = yes }`.
2. Check:
   - `is_councillor_of`;
   - the host's council window;
   - the seat after 30 and 365 days (engine re-validation and host AI reassignment);
   - the council modifier and a council task running;
   - the Kehillah's own council (V26), domicile, charter and appointment succession, all unchanged;
   - a clean `error.log`.
3. Repeat with `appoint_court_position` for `court_physician_court_position`, and with a stub mod
   court position.
4. **Kill the seated leader** and check that the community's succession runs clean. The
   implementation doc's §6 and §8 record that succession is the crash-prone subsystem.
5. Optional: designate the leader as diarch of a vizierate host.

**Route A** if steps 1–4 hold. Otherwise **Route B** (§5.1). Record the result in `docs/testing/`. The
spike is safe to run unattended; the slices after it wait for Daniel's review.

## 10. Build slices

1. **The office.**
   - Gates as scripted triggers, and the score and thresholds as script values.
   - The host decision, the petition, the yearly AI check, vacancy, host succession, and the 1066 seed.
   - The community-side contributors (§5.2), and the office shown on the Domain tab and the
     community's ledger row.
   - `events/kehillah_nagid_debug_events.txt`: set or clear the office, report each gate, force
     tenure.
2. **Service:** Route A or B, per the spike.
3. **Envy and the fall:** the danger component, dismissal under a hardening teaching, the fall and
   its realm-wide spike, the faction grievance.
4. **The realm's head:** the protection term for other communities, intercession, rite reception,
   Supplant the Nagid.
5. **Names and polish:** the §6 names, the game rule, the nickname, dilemma events.

Every mechanical effect is shown through modifiers, `effect_localization` and breakdown tooltips,
never in prose.
