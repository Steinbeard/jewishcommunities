# The Nagid

## 1. What Daniel asked for

## 2. Historical basis

Dates are for the writer. Verify them before citing in-game.

**Where the title was used.** *Nagid* ("leader, prince") was the Hebrew title of the recognised head
of the Jews of a Muslim realm. It is attested in Kairouan, Muslim Spain, Egypt and Yemen. In Egypt
the government post was *ra'is al-yahud*, "head of the Jews", and the two titles merged.

| Who | Where, when | Office at court | How he got the title |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hasdai ibn Shaprut | Córdoba, c. 940–970 | Physician to Abd al-Rahman III; diplomat (Byzantium, Queen Toda and Sancho of León) | Called *ha-Nasi*. He did the Nagid's work without the title |
| Abraham ibn Ata | Kairouan, early 11th c. | Physician to the Zirids Badis and al-Mu'izz | An honorific from Hai Gaon, 1015 (*negid ha-golah*) |
| Samuel ibn Naghrillah | Granada, 1027–1056 | Vizier of Habbus and Badis; commanded Granada's army | Title conferred 1027 |
| Joseph ibn Naghrillah | Granada, 1056–1066 | Vizier of Badis | Succeeded his father in both offices; killed 30 Dec 1066 |
| Judah b. Saadya, Mevorakh b. Saadya | Fatimid Egypt, from c. 1065 | Court physicians | Government office of head of the Jews (Cohen) |
| Maimonides; Abraham Maimonides | Ayyubid Egypt | Maimonides a physician in Saladin's circle; Abraham Nagid from 1205 | Maimonides' formal headship is debated. His descendants held the office for generations |
| Madmun b. Hasan | Aden, d. 1151 | Merchant representative | Nagid of Yemen |

**The seat was the ruler's city.**
- The office sat at Córdoba, Kairouan, Granada, Fustat beside Cairo, and Aden.
- The Exilarch sat at the caliph's Baghdad.
- The Rab de la Corte followed the Castilian court.
- Lucena, a largely Jewish town and a centre of learning, was never the seat; Granada was.

The pattern is that a man rose at court on personal skill (medicine, Arabic letters, finance,
diplomacy), and his court office made him the realm's Jewish head. The court came first.

**It was always the ruler's exception.** No faith permitted a Jew authority over believers:
- **Islam:** the jurists read the dhimma as forbidding a dhimmi authority over Muslims. Abu Ishaq's
  poem against Joseph makes exactly that case.
- **Latin Christianity:** church councils barred Jews from office long before Fourth Lateran (1215),
  canon 69. Gregory VII reportedly rebuked Alfonso VI in 1081 for letting Jews hold authority over
  Christians, and Alfonso kept Cidellus (Joseph ibn Ferrizuel) as his physician. *Verify the letter
  before citing it.*

Rulers made the exception anyway, and the backlash was the price.

**Once begun, it lasted:**
- Egypt's office lasted until the Ottoman conquest of 1517.
- Castile appointed a Rab de la Corte from the mid-13th century until 1492. The last, Abraham Seneor,
  converted shortly before the expulsion.
- England's Presbyter ran from 1199 to the expulsion of 1290.
- The Exilarchate outlived its own lapses.

The office survived rulers who were indifferent to the Jews, because it was useful: someone had to
answer for the community and its taxes.

**Were there Ashkenazi Nagidim? No.** The title was never used in Ashkenaz, and its communities
governed themselves through local leaders, ordinances and synods. Christian rulers did create
crown-appointed heads of "their" Jews, and the communities resisted them:
- **England, the *Presbyter Judaeorum*, 1199–1290:** a crown appointee tied to the Exchequer of the
  Jews.
- **The Empire, the *Hochmeister*, 1407:** King Rupert named a chief rabbi of the German Jews,
  identified with Israel of Krems. The purpose was mainly fiscal. Communities threatened him with
  excommunication, and the office soon lapsed.
- **Christian Iberia, the *Rab de la Corte* (Castile) and *Arrabi Mor* (Portugal):** they divided
  taxes among the communities and supervised their leadership. Most were physicians, interpreters
  or fiscal agents, and few were noted scholars.

**What made Muslim realms different:**
- The dhimma system dealt with each protected community through a recognised head, such as the
  Exilarch or the Nestorian Catholicos.
- The Islamicate courts employed dhimmi secretaries and physicians as a matter of course.

The headship was native to the dhimma; only the vizierate stretched it. Latin Christendom handled
"the king's Jews" through his treasury, so a Jewish head of all a realm's Jews came later and less
often.

## 3. Three layers: faith, realm, ruler

The mod already has a separate layer for each question:

  - a grievance for V31 §7's vassal faction, once it is built.
- **Tolerance traditions halve the cost** (§5).
- **Scope:** only Favored is charged. Tolerated under a strict teaching is unchanged, so this doesn't

  class, through effect_localization rather than prose.

## 4. The seat of the realm's Jews (every realm)

**The seat** is the community in the **capital county** of an independent ruler of duke tier or
higher (`capital_county`). There is one per realm, whatever the law. The seat holds the leadership
role in practice: it speaks for the realm's communities, and it lives at the ruler's door.

| Term | Seat in a duchy | Kingdom | Empire | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prosperity contributor | +5 | +10 | +15 | Court demand: purchases, loans, physicians, translators |
| Greatness contributor | +5 | +10 | +15 | Patronage; scholars drawn to court |
| Danger component "At the ruler's door" | + | ++ | ++ | Court intrigue, the capital's crowd; Granada 1066 |

- **The seat is the first target of the lord's demands** (V36 "The Lord's Demands" weights it first).
- **The seat can intercede in the crises of the realm's other communities** (accusations and the
  lord's demands): a weak voice. The Nagid's is a strong one (§9).
- **The lord's own lands.** A community in a county the ruler holds directly, other than the capital,
  gets a small Stability contributor, "Under the lord's own hand". It has one master, and his
  protection is closest.
- **No general realm-tier term elsewhere.** County development already pays a major city up to +15
  Prosperity (`kehillah_bd_urban_opportunity_prosperity_value`). The seat terms are about the court,
  not the market, and scaling only the seat keeps the effect easy to read.
- **Capital moves:** the seat moves with the capital. The old seat loses the role, and a realm whose
  new capital has no community has no seat until one is founded there (§6's invitation).
- All values are script values and starting points, like V20's other contributors.

## 5. Culture in the outlook

Today the outlook (`kehillah_outlook_on_jews_value`) has no culture source. Vanilla's African
Tolerance gives +10 opinion of other faiths and −25% unhappiness in counties of other faiths, but
it never reaches the outlook, which drives the policy review, protection and stance.

- **A new outlook source,** `kehillah_outlook_culture_value`, shown as a "Culture" line in the
  breakdown. It follows the same regent redirect as the other sources:

  | Tradition | Outlook | Halves "beyond the teaching" costs | Vanilla 1066 holders of note |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | African Tolerance | +10 | yes | Maghrebi (the Zirid Nagid of Kairouan), Baranis, Zaghawa |
  | Steppe Tolerance | +10 | yes | Khazar, Uyghur, Karluk, Kerait and others |
  | Religion Blending | +5 | yes | Yemeni (the Nagid of Yemen), Sogdian, Gujarati and others |
  | Xenophilic | +5 | no (it concerns foreign cultures, not faiths) | Sicilian (Norman Sicily), Polish (Kalisz, 1264) |

- **No double count in opinion.** `kehillah_jews_opinion_value` turns the outlook into opinion.
  It subtracts the culture line for traditions whose vanilla effect already raises opinion of other
  faiths (African Tolerance, +10). Check the other three traditions' vanilla modifiers when building.

**The rename.** The display names change; the law keys (`kehillah_jewish_settlement_*`) stay, so
saves and script are unaffected.

| Key | Old name | New name |
|---|---|---|
| (group) | Jewish Settlement Policy | **Status of the Jews** |
| `_banned` | Banned | **Banned** |
| `_discouraged` | Discouraged | **Restricted** |
| `_allowed` | Allowed | **Tolerated** |
| `_encouraged` | Encouraged | **Favored** |

All other rules stay as built: one rung at a time, the five-year cooldown, and piety to raise
(scaled by §3). The rename is a localization pass across charters, news, the policy map mode, the
realm view and tooltips.

**Invite Jewish Settlers.** Favored no longer opens the invitation by itself:
- **The decision** is shown while the realm is Favored. It opens the existing 20-year invitation
  (`kehillah_invitation_open`) aimed at one county, by default the capital. That gives the realm
  a seat and, in time, a Nagid. The aimed county takes V34's +8 invited weight. The invitation's
  20 years are the decision's cooldown.
- **The AI** takes it when Favored, weighted by:
  - stewardship and ambition ("towns and markets to fill");
  - a capital without a community;
  - greed.
- **The open-door review** (V34 §7) raises the law and takes the decision in one step, so its
  behaviour is unchanged. The Norman Conquest decision ([2026-10-29 revision](2026-10-29-conquest-invitation-revision.md))
  calls the decision's effect directly with its English letter.
- **Lowering the law** from Favored still withdraws an open invitation.
- **Old saves:** invitations already open stay open.

## 7. The office: founding and keeping it

**The office is an institution of the realm.** It is a variable on the ruler's primary title,
`kehillah_nagid_office`, with its founding date. It survives successions, and it is carried over
when the primary title changes.

**Founding: "Establish the Office of the Nagid"** (the name varies, §13). Requirements:
- **The realm is Favored.** *Daniel.*
- **The seat community's Standing is at least Flourishing** (700). *Daniel*: high standing.
- **The ruler is a Protector** (`kehillah_outlook_protector_trigger`, outlook 30 or more).
- **The seat's leader qualifies** (§8).

Founding also makes the seat's leader the first Nagid. The player-community path is the seat
leader's petition, "Ask the Lord to Establish the Office". The AI ruler answers on its outlook and the
§11 weights.

**Keeping it: stickiness** (*Daniel*). The office persists while:
- the realm is **Tolerated or Favored**;
- its teaching is not Exclusion or Abolition.

It does **not** need Favored or a Protector to persist, so it survives indifferent and cold rulers.
Without a seat (the capital has no community, or has just moved), the office is **dormant**: kept
but vacant, and refilled when a seat exists. Dropping to Restricted or Banned, or adopting Exclusion
or Abolition, **abolishes** it.

**Why a cold ruler keeps it.** It pays. While the office is filled, the realm's communities pay a
higher V31 §8 tribute (the Nagid as fiscal agent), and each new ruler collects a confirmation fee
(§8).

**"Abolish the Office."** A ruler who is Cold or worse can end it by decision:
- he loses the tribute bonus and some prestige ("breaking a custom of the realm");
- he gains piety under a strict or exclusionary teaching;
- the realm's communities take a Stability hit.

The AI weighs it by the Convinced stance, zeal and the teaching.

## 8. The holder

- **The Nagid is always the seat community's leader** (*agreed*). The ruler decides whether to found
  and confirm the office, not which community it goes to. The person comes from the seat
  community's own appointment succession, which is merit-based (v3). The first draft's "Supplant
  the Nagid" rivalry between communities is dropped. Rivalry now happens inside the seat's own
  succession.
- **Qualified:** an adult who is free, Jewish and holds no other such office. Their best of
  diplomacy, stewardship and learning is 12 or more (tunable). The seat's Standing is at least
  Healthy (400) to hold the office, against Flourishing to found it.
- **Confirmation.** On founding, and after each change of seat leader or of ruler, the ruler gets a
  confirmation event:
  - **Confirm:** the community pays the **confirmation fee**. This is V31 §8.2 part 3, unbuilt
    until now. It is larger under a Predator or a cold ruler.
  - **Withhold:** the office stays vacant with an acting head. The ruler is asked again yearly.
  - The AI weighs outlook, greed (the fee and the tribute bonus) and opinion of the leader. Even a
    cold, greedy ruler usually confirms.
- **The ruler's outlook shapes the tenure,** not whether the office exists. It sets:
  - the fee;
  - how often the dilemmas of §10.2 come;
  - the chance of dismissal.
- **An unqualified seat leader**, such as a child or a weak scholar, leaves the office vacant with an
  acting head until a qualified leader follows.

## 9. What the office does

**Service at court: the core ask.** The Nagid can be appointed to the seats in §3's table. **Whether
the engine allows this is the open risk; §14 decides it.**

- **Route A, preferred: a real seat. RULED OUT by the 2026-10-30 spike (§14):** the engine refuses
  any council seat, court position or diarchy to a character who is not the host's courtier or
  vassal. The design is kept for the record: the Nagid would have held a vanilla council seat or
  court position of the host. That comes with:
  - the host's council UI, council tasks and the position's modifiers;
  - the outlook's existing "A Jew in his service" +10 (`kehillah_outlook_court_value`), which then
    fires on its own;
  - a mod court position, `kehillah_nagid_court_position`, which would show the office itself on
    the host's court screen.

  For a Muslim host with a vizierate diarchy, the Nagid as diarch is the closest model of Samuel's
  office. This is a stretch goal.
- **Route B, fallback: service in name.** The host picks a service, Vizier, Treasurer, Physician or
  Envoy. The host then gets a modifier scaled by the Nagid's matching skill, the way council
  modifiers scale.
- **Rejected:**
  - making the leader the host's vassal (V32: landless vassals need an administrative liege, and the
    charter and appointment succession rest on independence);
  - moving the leader into the host's court, which would end their rule of the community.

**For the Nagid's community**, on top of the seat terms (§4):
- Greatness and Stability contributors, recorded in the breakdown (V20's one source of truth).
- A Nagid term in the lord's protection during violence (`kehillah_host_protection_mult_value`).
- Monthly prestige and influence.
- The nickname "ha-Nagid" (`nick_kehillah_ha_nagid`, existing) after ten years in the Nagid-named
  form of the office.

**For the realm's other communities:**
- **"Under the Nagid's protection":** a small protection term.
- **Strong intercession** in their accusation and demand crises.
- **Reception by rite.** Communities of Minhag Ashkenaz (V45), other than the seat, take a small
  Stability penalty, "a head set over us by the crown". This follows the 1407 resistance.

**For the host:**
- a capable servant;
- the tribute bonus and confirmation fee (§7, §8);
- a small outlook term while the Nagid is in favour.

## 10. The cost: envy, dilemmas and the fall

Joseph's death in 1066 is the anchor.

### 10.1 Envy at court

While a Nagid holds a seat with authority (Vizier, Chancellor, Steward, Marshal), a **Nagid's envy**
term grows with tenure, up to a cap. It adds to the seat's "At the ruler's door" danger for every
community in the realm, and gives V31 §7's faction a grievance. A physician-only Nagid draws much
less envy, so the player chooses between influence and safety.

### 10.2 Dilemmas and clergy pressure

- **Dilemmas:** an extraordinary levy on the realm's Jews, a loan on bad terms, acting against a rival
  courtier. Each answer trades the host's opinion against Stability.
- **Clergy pressure:** under a strict teaching (Strict Pact, Separation and Mission), the clergy's
  agenda includes "no Jew in office". A yearly chance of an event presses the ruler to dismiss the
  Nagid:
  - a protective ruler refuses, at a cost in piety and the clergy's opinion;
  - otherwise the holder is dismissed, but **the office persists**, vacant until the next
    confirmation.

  V40 §5.3's caliph decree, "dismissal of dhimmis from office", uses the same effect when built.

### 10.3 The fall

A fall comes from:
- the host's opinion of the Nagid falling to −40 or below;
- a rival's scheme;
- a crisis gone badly.

It dismisses the Nagid and may also:
- confiscate gold;
- open V32's arrest;
- add a **"the Nagid has fallen"** danger spike, lasting several years, to every community in the
  realm.

## 11. Why Muslim realms get Nagids and Christian ones rarely do

The bias lives in the office's own rules, not in a general outlook bonus. The outlook also drives
pogrom protection and the policy reviews, so a general bonus would make Muslim realms friendlier
everywhere.

1. **Starting seeds** (§13): Granada, Fatimid Egypt and Baghdad's Exilarchate. **Stickiness** (§7)
   keeps them through indifferent rulers.
2. **Dhimma halves the cost** of Favored (§3).
3. **AI weights to found the office:**
   - heritages: Arabic, Berber, Iranian, Iberian and other Middle Eastern and North African heritages;
   - caliphs;
   - stewardship;
   - tolerance traditions.

   A Latin AI ruler founds it only with a revenue motive (greed or high stewardship, the Presbyter
   and Hochmeister pattern), which is rare.
4. **The Ashkenaz reception penalty** (§9) makes the office cost more where Ashkenazi communities
   live.

**Expected result** (to check in the soak, §15): Muslim and Iberian courts usually have the office.
In Latin realms it is rare, and it comes late.

**Optional game rule:** *Nagid office: Dynamic* (default, the rules above), *Historical* (only
rulers of Middle Eastern, North African and Iberian heritage may found it), or *Off*.

## 12. The Exilarch: the same office as a special case (*Daniel*)

The Exilarch uses everything above with four differences:

| | Nagid | Exilarch |
|---|---|---|
| Host | The independent ruler | **The caliph, as head of faith**, even as a vassal. In vanilla 1066 al-Qa'im holds `d_sunni` (Sunni head of faith), but `d_baghdad`'s liege is `k_persia`, so the ordinary rule would put the seat in the Seljuk capital |
| Seat | The ruler's capital | **The caliph's capital**, Baghdad: v47's "the Kehillah of Baghdad *is* the Exilarchate" |
| Holder | The seat's qualified leader | **A Ben David**, v47's strong appointment preference, not a hard filter. Without one, the office is vacant and the community is led without the style |
| Reach | The ruler's realm | **Realms of the caliph's faith that have no Nagid of their own** |

Name: **Exilarch** (*Resh Galuta*). As in v47, there is no separate title. This supersedes v47's
build-order item 3 in mechanism, while keeping its decisions.

## 14. Slice 0: the engine spike (do this first)

Vanilla fills council seats from courtiers and vassals (`valid_character` reads
`root.liege_or_court_owner`). Court positions are scored "per eligible courtier/vassal"
(`_court_positions.info`). A Kehillah leader is an independent landless ruler with
`cannot_be_vassal_or_liege`, so neither pool contains them.

The spike tests whether the effects bypass the pool. Through `ck3_script`, in a 1066 debug game:
1. As Badis, run `assign_councillor_type = { type = councillor_chancellor target = <Granada leader>
   remove_existing_councillor = yes }`.
2. Check:
   - `is_councillor_of`;
   - the host's council window;
   - the seat after 30 and 365 days (engine re-validation and host AI reassignment);
   - the council modifier and a council task running;
   - the Kehillah's own council (V26), domicile, charter and appointment succession, all unchanged;
   - a clean `error.log`.
3. Repeat with `appoint_court_position` for `court_physician_court_position`, and with a stub mod
   court position.
4. **Kill the seated leader** and check that the community's succession runs clean. The
   implementation doc's §6 and §8 record that succession is the crash-prone subsystem.
5. Confirm `capital_county` for Badis, al-Mustansir and Tamim in 1066, and confirm that al-Qa'im

## 16. What changed from the first draft (commit cee2173)

| First draft | Now |
|---|---|
| The teaching was the gate: Dhimma and Witness "allowed" the office, stricter teachings forbade it | No teaching permits it. **Favored** is the gate. The teaching sets its **cost** (§3) |
| Settlement Allowed or Encouraged sufficed | **Favored** to found the office; **Tolerated** or better to keep it (§7) |
| The ruler chose any qualified leader in the realm | The **seat** (the capital's community) supplies the Nagid. The ruler founds and confirms (§4, §8) |
| No capital or tier effect | Seat contributors and danger by tier, plus the lord's-lands term (§4) |
| The office ended with the ruler's favour | A **sticky institution** that survives cold rulers. Clergy pressure removes the holder, not the office (§7, §10.2) |
| "Supplant the Nagid" rivalry | Dropped; rivalry is inside the seat's own succession |
| Culture: AI weight and names only | Plus an outlook **culture line** and halved leniency costs (§5) |
| Encouraged invited settlers automatically | Favored unlocks **Invite Jewish Settlers** (§6) |
| No Exilarch | The **Exilarch** is a special case of the same office (§12) |
