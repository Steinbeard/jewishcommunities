# Neighbouring community crisis aid

A crisis should be an event in a regional Jewish network, not only in the community under attack. The existing settlement-network definition is reused: a neighbouring community is another registered Kehillah within vanilla's `squared_distance_medium` band. This is deliberately geographic rather than based on a shared ruler, so an imperial realm does not turn into one implausibly instant relief network.

## Sequence

1. When a crisis enters Agitation, every eligible neighbouring leader receives *News from [Kehillah]*. They may send tzedakah, proclaim a fast, send their Shtadlan to advocate, or decline. Tzedakah transfers a small amount of gold to the threatened leader, grants the giver piety and Greatness, creates a ten-year grateful-opinion tie, and adds only five points to the threatened community's Spared race. Prayer and advocacy have smaller race effects.
2. Once the host ruler has given the political settlement, those same neighbours receive *Word from [Kehillah]*. The report differentiates an averted threat, a defended quarter, a bought-off host, a struck quarter, and expulsion.
3. A spared/held/bought-off community can receive a confidence-restoring gift or thanksgiving. A struck or expelled community can receive rebuilding funds, an offer of temporary shelter for displaced households, or a public fast. Relief and shelter directly advance the existing Recovery Endure track if it remains open.

The offer of shelter is intentionally represented as temporary refuge and recovery capacity, not as an automatic permanent migration. Population transfer needs a dedicated migration/refugee system before it can safely change community membership, titles, and domiciles.

## No hook for tzedakah

Tzedakah creates a durable grateful-opinion modifier (+20, decaying over ten years), while prayer creates a smaller +8 modifier. It does **not** create a weak hook: routine communal charity should create reciprocal trust and memory, not a legally enforceable personal claim. A later covenant, loan, or negotiated resettlement can use hooks where an explicit obligation is part of the fiction.
