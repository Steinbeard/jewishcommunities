# Kashrut

## 1. What 1.21 offers

1.21 added a faith doctrine group, `dietary_restrictions`. Its doctrines work through parameters that vanilla holding
buildings read, from their **county's** rite, to choose what food their province asks of the market:

| Parameter | Effect on food needs |
|---|---|
| `cereals_forbidden` | meat instead of cereals |
| `meat_forbidden` | no meat |
| `fish_permitted` | fish allowed |
| `diet_needs_comforts` | comforts instead of meat |
| others | similar substitutions |

The goods involved:

- **meat:** meat and fish;
- **comforts:** cheese, fruit, oil, honey and tea;
- **separately:** cattle and salt.

Every Jewish rite had **No Dietary Restrictions** ("eating other living things is not a problem").

## 2. The problem: where Jews live

Kehillot are domiciles in Christian and Muslim counties. A doctrine alone would change food needs only in Jewish-rite
counties, such as Khazaria and Beta Israel lands, or Jewish realms later (Track B). Nowhere else would a player see it.
Kashrut has to show where Jews actually live: in the **Quarter**, and in what the community trades with its town.

## 3. Representing kosher meat

Kosher meat is not something a market sells. The community's own shochet slaughters a **live beast** of a permitted
kind. In 1.21's goods, that is **cattle**, not market meat (which, generically, includes swine).

So, in one mechanic, **kashrut makes a community buy cattle instead of meat**. That is also what makes kosher meat
dearer: the quarter adds its own demand for cattle to the town's market. Fish with fins and scales stays permitted.
Wine for Kiddush was already one of the Quarter's needs.

## 4. As built (2026-11-23, simplified the same day)

- **The doctrine:** **Kashrut** (`doctrine_diet_kashrut`, in `common/religion/doctrine_types/kehillah_kashrut_doctrine.txt`)
  replaces No Dietary Restrictions on every Jewish rite and faith:
  - Rabbinism and the three minhagim;
  - Yordei Merkavah;
  - Karaites;
  - Kabars;
  - Samaritans;
  - Beta Israel;
  - Cochin.
- **Who can pick it:** only a Jewish faith (`can_pick`, `religion_tag = judaism_religion`; `can_pick` runs in faith scope).
- **Parameters:**
  - `meat_permitted` and `leather_permitted`, so vanilla holdings in Jewish counties eat normally;
  - `kehillah_kashrut`, whose tooltip line reads "A Jewish quarter buys cattle for its shochet rather than meat".
- **The Quarter** (`kehillah_jewish_quarter_01`) asks the town market for **cattle**, beside wine, books and cloth.
  Met, it pays the host a little more artisan tax.
- **The market line** (`kehillah_bd_market_supply_prosperity_value`) counts cattle for a community that keeps kashrut
  (`kehillah_keeps_kashrut_trigger`). The line became a bonus only on the same day (section 7).

**Simplified (Daniel, the same day: "too convoluted").** The first cut also had:

- a salt need for koshering;
- a separate "kosher table" Affluence line;
- the slaughterhouse selling trefot and hindquarters into the town as meat;
- a 2% cattle price modifier for Jewish characters.

All four were removed: one good, one existing line.

## 7. No penalty for a missing good (2026-11-23)

Daniel asked whether a community should be penalized for having no cattle when other food is available. It should
not:

- **Geography:** cattle is a regional 1.21 trade good, and its absence from a market does not mean a town had no
  livestock.
- **Other food:** fish is always permitted, and for most Jews meat was a food of Shabbat and festivals.
- **No lever:** the player cannot add a good to a town's market.

Daniel found the other market penalties harsh as well. Worms sat at -45 at the 1066 start. So the whole market line is
now **a bonus only**:

- each of the quarter's needs the market supplies adds 5 Affluence: wine, books and paper, cloth, and cattle for a
  community that keeps kashrut;
- that makes at most 20;
- a missing good costs nothing.

It reads "Goods our quarter needs, sold in the town's market: +N". The quarter still asks for cattle, so kashrut still
raises local demand for cattle and pays the host when met.

## 8. The quarter's needs follow vanilla's pattern (2026-11-23)

Daniel: the quarter was not integrating with 1.21's resources the way vanilla districts do, and asked why cloth.

- **What vanilla does:**
  - districts that house people ask for categories, any good of which will do. Vanilla's religious community district
    asks for comforts and clothes;
  - specific goods appear only as production inputs, or for parameter-driven religious flavor, such as communion wine
    and the diet doctrines.
- **What the quarter had:** four specific goods. Cloth had no strong reason.
- **What it asks now:**
  - **comforts**, a category: the community's table;
  - **books**, a category, vanilla's: its learning;
  - **wine**, specific: Kiddush, as communion wine is for a temple;
  - **cattle**, specific: kashrut, in place of a meat need, as the diet doctrines swap foods.

  Textiles are dropped.
- **The Affluence line** gives 5 for each need the market meets, at most 20, and reads category needs through
  `trade_category_unfulfilled_need`.
- **Why kashrut is always on:** vanilla would key the cattle swap to the county's rite. A Jewish quarter stands in
  another rite's county, so the swap is always on.

**Live-tested 2026-11-23** (screenshots `04`, `05`):

- Worms's quarter lists comforts and books as categories, then wine and cattle, with no textiles;
- Worms's market meets all four, so the line reads `"Goods our quarter needs, sold in the town's market: +20`";
- `error.log` shows nothing from the change, and the `can_pick` scope error is gone.

The shortfalls seen at an earlier boot did not recur. The bonus-only rule was checked by reading only, since no
shortfall came up.

## 9. What the doctrine does, and the Jewish rites (2026-11-23)

Daniel asked what the doctrine actually does, and whether the quarter's needs are required or only desired.

**Needs are desired, not required**, as in vanilla:

- **Met:** a need the town market meets pays the host a little artisan tax, and the community +5 Affluence.
- **Unmet:** nothing is lost.

The doctrine is separate from the quarter, because a quarter sits in another rite's county.

**In a Jewish-rite county** (Khazaria, Beta Israel lands, Track B realms), vanilla holdings read the county's rite:

- **Diet:** with `meat_permitted` and `leather_permitted`, they eat as under No Dietary Restrictions.
- **Removed: `fish_permitted`.** It was in the first cut and was a bug. Vanilla gives it only to doctrines that forbid
  meat, and castles then add a separate fish food need beside the meat need.

**For a Kehillah** the doctrine is:

- its identity in the faith view;
- the condition for counting cattle in the market bonus (`kehillah_keeps_kashrut_trigger`).

**Rites:** all ten Jewish rites carry Kashrut:

- Rabbinism, shown as Minhag Bavel;
- the minhagim of Ashkenaz, Sepharad and Yerushalmi;
- Yordei Merkavah;
- Karaites, Kabars, Samaritans, Beta Israel and Cochin.

The five single-rite faiths also list it at faith level. The Rabbinic faith keeps rite doctrines on its rites, as its
file records. Diet is a rite doctrine in 1.21; its cost checks `rite_has_doctrine`. Dynamic rites inherit it from their
founder's rite.

## 10. Flavor in Jewish-ruled lands (2026-11-23)

Daniel: the 1% fish bonus was silly. `fish_in_demand` is removed.

Kashrut now shows in what a Jewish ruler's land raises:

- **Herds for the shochet:** cattle production +15%.
- **No swine kept:** meat production -10%. 1.21's generic meat includes pork.

These sit in the doctrine's `character_modifier`, so they show in the doctrine's tooltip. Production tags in a character
modifier reach the holder's domain, as vanilla's merchant company buildings use them. They also apply to Kehillah
leaders and Jewish merchants, but only to what those characters produce.

## 11. Salt, not production (2026-11-23)

Daniel: vanilla's diet doctrines are about consumption, not production. So section 10's cattle +15% / meat -10%
production modifier is removed.

**Rejected: making meat dearer.** It would draw ordinary meat from anywhere into Jewish lands, the one thing a kosher
table cannot use, because price signals move goods.

**Kept: the quarter's cattle swap.** Its pull is historically right: live beasts brought to the shochet.

**Chosen: salt.** Meat is salted to draw out its blood (*melichah*), so the doctrine carries
`trade_good_salt_sell_price_mult = 0.02`:

- **Vanilla's pattern:** 1.21 tenets mark the goods their practice values with a small sell-price modifier in
  `character_modifier`. `tenet_gruesome_festivals` gives cattle +2%; `tenet_esotericism` gives glass +1.25%.
- **No distortion:** a price on salt draws only salt, so it brings no non-kosher meat into Jewish lands.

The console scripts `kk2`-`kk4` tested the removed production modifier and are now only of historical use.

## 12. Kiddush wine and books in Jewish-ruled counties (2026-11-23)

Daniel: should the Jewish tenets have resource needs, as vanilla's do? In vanilla 1.21, a tenet's parameter makes the
temple and religious community districts of its rite's counties want a specific good. The mod now reuses vanilla's own
parameters, so vanilla's districts respond with no file overrides. Their tooltip lines are neutral.

- **Kiddush wine:** `communion_wine`, on **Covenant Community** (seven of the ten Jewish rites) and on **Sacred
  Liturgy** (Sepharad, Yordei Merkavah). Temple and religious community districts want wine, granting popular opinion
  when met.
- **Books for learning:** `tenet_legalism_temple_district_control`, on **Scholarly Study** (Rabbinism, Ashkenaz).
  Temple districts want Paper & Books, granting control when met. The Karaites and Kabars already had it through
  vanilla Legalism.

These reach only Jewish-ruled counties. Kehillot are served by the quarter, which already wants wine and books. The
Karaites are not excluded from wine; the question was raised and left as is.

## 13. The Temple's offerings (2026-11-23)

**Vanilla 1.21 has the rebuilt Temple.** `temple_in_jerusalem_01` is the Dome of the Rock's upgrade, and only a Jewish
holder can build it (1,000 gold and 1,000 piety). Both buildings want **incense** and **oil**. Neither asks for the
offerings.

- **The override:** `common/buildings/kehillah_temple_in_jerusalem_override.txt` is a by-name copy of the 1.21 block. It
  adds needs for **cattle** (the offerings, +5% monthly piety per fill, up to 3), **wine** (libations, +2%, up to 2)
  and **grain** (the showbread, +2%). Unmet needs cost nothing. The Temple's existence is the switch, and it stands
  only at the holy site. Rebase it on each patch.
- **Bug fixed:** the mod's Temple-service check (`kehillah_temple_service_available_trigger`) and the Restore the Temple
  Service decision required `has_building = dome_of_the_rock_01`. That matches the Dome only, so building vanilla's
  Temple, which replaces the Dome, would have ended the service. Both now use `has_building_or_higher`.
- **Mount Gerizim** has no vanilla building. A Samaritan equivalent is left for later.

## 14. Matching vanilla's format (2026-11-23)

Daniel compared the Kashrut tooltip with vanilla's Flesh Alone. Vanilla diets show only short parameter lines, and a
one- or two-sentence description in the believers' voice. Ours had an `"All Jews`" character-modifier block, and a
description with a mistake.

- **The description** is now in the believers' voice and says only what every Jewish rite shares: clean beasts, rightly
  slaughtered and drained of blood; fish with fins and scales. The meat-and-milk rule in its full sense, and koshering by
  salt, are rabbinic. Karaites and Samaritans also carry this doctrine.
- **The effect lines** follow vanilla's phrasing:
  - `"The Jewish Quarter gains a building need for Cattle, granting its community Affluence when met`";
  - `"Sell price for Salt increased by 1% in a market with a Jewish Quarter`".
- **Salt moved** from the doctrine's `character_modifier` (+2%, the `"All Jews`" block) to the quarter building's
  `market_modifier` (+1%, vanilla's in-demand size). It is shown through a parameter, `kehillah_kashrut_salt`.

## 15. Hunted game (2026-11-23)

Daniel asked whether kashrut should affect eating from the hunt. A beast brought down in the chase is never ritually
slaughtered, so it is not kosher, whatever the animal. Vanilla only stops Jews and Muslims eating **boar**
(`can_eat_boar_trigger` in `hunt.4004`, "Felling a Beast").

- `hunt.4004` is overridden by id (`id_override_priority`), generated by `scripts/kehillah_build_kosher_overrides.py`
  into `events/activities/hunt_activity/kehillah_kosher_hunt_events.txt`, with two changes:
  - a kashrut-keeper is never added to `meat_eaters`, the guests who share the kill;
  - the host's "We eat it here and now!" is hidden for a kashrut-keeper.
- **In its place**, the same host (gluttonous or comfort eater) may send the meat to the poor, for minor piety. Nobody
  vanilla would not have offered the feast gets a new choice, so trophy choices and AI weights elsewhere are untouched.
