# Florin: trade, credit, and home communities

## 1. What 1.21 actually supplies

## 2. The play loop

The Kehillah leads a congregation and protects its common treasury. A merchant company belongs to a character and moves goods. The player may cultivate **home-community merchants** as partners: a merchant brings information, goods, transport, donations, and contacts; the community offers kin, local introductions, arbitration, shelter, books, and a place to return. Partnership creates mutual opportunity and mutual exposure. It must be possible to play a scholarly or political community without running a trade network.

### 2.1 A merchant's home community

- A Jewish adventurer or Merchant may ask one existing Jewish Kehillah to be their **home community**. The leader may welcome, decline, or set conditions after an actual visit or introduction. Faith or culture alone does not assign a home. One character/company has at most one home at a time; a community may welcome several, with diminishing mechanical benefit after the first three active companies.
- Store the relationship on the merchant's durable company title when one exists, with a character fallback for an adventurer. Store the reverse title list on the Kehillah for discovery, not as a second authority. On a merchant-government transition, transfer the character fallback to the company; on company succession, the new head inherits the relationship but may renew or end it. A former member, exile, converted character, dissolved community, or relocated Quarter gets an explicit reconciliation path. Historical or generated Jewish merchants are **not** assigned a Kehillah by blanket seeding.
- A home link grants limited access: a community visit from anywhere on the merchant's route, a local-contact boost to a route that actually stops in the home's Market, and a small Greatness/Prosperity contribution to the community only while the merchant is active and trusted. It does not grant a free route, monopoly, trade post, or passive share of every distant profit.
- The merchant can sponsor a Quarter project, carry a sefer or scholar, relay an invitation to refugees, or petition the host for safe passage. The community can send a Dayyan to arbitrate a disputed partnership, recommend a trustworthy factor, give relief after a route is disrupted, or refuse to endorse a predatory merchant. Donations transfer real Gold and have a cooldown; route activity alone never mints Gold for the Kehillah.
- A merchant may break with the community over a commercial dispute, a public usury scandal, a hostile host charter, or failure of protection. Repair is a character interaction with reciprocal consent. Loyalty should be earned rather than an unconditional same-faith bonus.

The existing adventurer **Visit the Jewish Community** bridge is the entry point for adventurers. Merchants need their own narrow visit/introduction path because the vanilla adventurer decision does not include merchant government. Reuse the current household recruitment and invitation patterns; do not override another large vanilla GUI for this feature.

## 3. Loans, doctrine, and policy

### 3.1 Should the Kehillah lend directly?

**Yes: it already does.** `kehillah_loan_contract` originates a loan from the community leader's treasury, records the borrower in `kehillah_loan_debtors`, and the borrower repays through `kehillah_repay_loan_decision`. Keep the negotiated task contract, as V8 chose; it makes the loan a relationship with the communal institution. The merchant's native **Loan Gold** interaction remains a personal company loan. Label lender identity and who bears a loss in every UI: **Kehillah treasury** versus **merchant company**. Never count a merchant's private capital as communal Gold or cancel it with the mod's `Forgive the Debt` lever.

**First 1.21 slice:** leave existing community loans and saves intact; make doctrine and charter text visible on the contract, then add a shared read-only credit exposure helper. A later backend slice may call vanilla `set_up_new_loan_effect` from the contract's final challenge event so the standard story, multiple-creditor repayment, repudiation, and inheritance apply. That requires a save migration, title/character lender ownership decision, and tests of every existing default, forgiveness, expulsion, succession, and crisis hook. Never write both ledgers for one new loan without one being an explicit non-authoritative migration mirror. An existing save must never lose a community receivable because its borrower now has a native story.

Vanilla's open interest is 25%, disguised 15%, and an interest-free instrument is available (`gdp_loan_open_interest_rate_value`, `gdp_loan_disguised_interest_rate_value`). The mod's current repayment multiplier must be reconciled against those choices; do not simply add vanilla interest on top. Judaism's default **Outsiders Only** condemns an interest-bearing loan to another Jewish borrower even across Jewish rites (`gdp_usury_condemns_loan_trigger` compares religion). A non-Jewish borrower's own rite controls whether an openly written native bond can be voided. The charter may define secular protection or permitted forms, but must not erase the faith/cleric decision. Interest-free charity can be offered across rites and should strengthen Greatness or trust instead of being a money farm.

### 3.2 Realm lending policy and charter

Define a small **realm lending posture** for rulers hosting Kehillot: **Protected credit**, **Customary credit**, **Restricted credit**. It is a political/legal policy, not a replacement for usury doctrine. Protected lets communities register and enforce contracts and makes access to credit more reliable; Restricted limits new communal lending and may make a dispute or seizure more likely; Customary follows the present charter. As in V17, changing realm policy changes the frontier for future agreements and triggers a named charter response, not an instantaneous rewrite of every existing contract. The community's particular market-access and protection terms remain in the charter. A Jewish or non-Jewish ruler may choose any posture that law and existing content allow; do not hard-code religion as the policy.

The AI considers actual need (negative Gold, war expense, other available financiers), the community's liquid capital and trust, the borrower rite's usury position, prior repayment, host protection, and faction pressure. A ruler can value **access to an eligible Jewish source of capital**: a capped, visible positive outlook term only if a local/realm Kehillah is willing and solvent and a financing need exists. The term disappears when access closes or the need ends. A loan actually owed creates a separate, initially useful relationship and then the existing age/debt resentment; cap and display the net finance term so a large debt does not become a perpetual approval bonus. Other merchants and financial options count: the premise is access to scarce capital in a specific place, never that the ruler's non-Jewish subjects cannot lend by identity. Where the host owes several Jewish lenders, count the debtors and exposure without double counting a community's own loan.

The current `kehillah_outlook_debts_value` applies −5 after the first year and −15 after five years, with a further −10 for Greedy. `kehillah_outlook_character_own_value` adds +5 for a Greedy ruler only while he has no community debt. Those existing terms need a reviewed, non-stacking composition with the proposed access term. A ruler's outlook is character-wide, so the access term must require an actual community in his realm and avoid crediting an unrelated Jewish population elsewhere.

### 3.3 Exposure and violent politics

Introduce one read-only `kehillah_credit_exposure_value` per community, based on **outstanding principal/amount owed by debtors in the host realm**, scaled to each debtor's annual income and capped. Include the community's loans; include a home merchant's native story only if the merchant is publicly connected to this community and the debtor is in its realm. Show provenance and lender in tooltips. Only a public or exposed disguised bond should feed public agitation; the debtor's private incentive to escape a debt can exist before public knowledge. Track the stock, not cumulative lifetime loans, and reduce it promptly on repayment, forgiveness, repudiation, death transfer, or departure. No per-frame world scan: update/cache on loan lifecycle and a bounded periodic audit.

- **Debts and a Dear Year:** gate the natural opening on actual relevant outstanding debt, as ROADMAP already requests. Start from `kehillah_crisis_opening_pulse_effect` and `kehillah_crisis_debts_chance_value`. Scale the *chance* modestly with exposure and the number of interested local debtors; war may still raise it. A debug event may bypass the natural gate. The debt's size should also change debtor-specific catalyst weights, not turn every ordinary arrear into a massacre.
- **Debtor politics:** a debtor vassal or town notable who would benefit from cancellation gets a bounded increase in hostility, faction recruitment, and willingness to support a debt-burning demand. A debtor host's need for credit can instead motivate protection while repayment seems possible. Poverty, war, repudiation opportunity, and a hostile teaching can shift the balance toward cancellation. The incident must name the creditor and the debt at stake, with a humane nonviolent resolution path: extension, partial settlement, forgiveness, mediation, or an enforceable guarantee.
- **Crisis outcomes:** if debt records are burned or a loan is forgiven, cancel only the identified instruments and update the authoritative ledger/story; account for actual loss to communal Prosperity or merchant Gold. A merchant creditor may protest, negotiate, or withdraw his home link. A Kehillah cannot forgive a private company loan. A legal repudiation by the borrower is a different outcome from mob destruction or a community's voluntary forgiveness.
- **Ceilings:** cap exposure's contribution to crisis opening and faction weights, preserve existing cooldowns, and require the normal crisis danger/incident process. Greater lending should produce interesting vulnerability, not a deterministic pogrom or a universal penalty to Jews.

The current danger code counts +15 Greatness per active community debtor (up to +60), whereas the natural debt opening has only a flat 0.5% quarterly chance, or 2% in a host war. The first implementation should instrument these existing values and compare before/after odds in a debug probe; it should not stack a second unbounded danger bonus on the existing one.

## 4. Trade networks and districts

The first integration reads the **Market containing the Quarter's county** and the actual routes serving it. A healthy local market and fulfilled food or book/paper needs may support Prosperity or Greatness; a shortage, interruption, siege, or road loss may create a relief or trade opportunity. Put the bounded market input in the existing `kehillah_bd_local_economy_value` rather than inventing a fourth pillar or paying a second profit stream. 1.21 renames county development to prosperity; the 1.21 port must settle that compatibility before this formula changes. A Jewish home merchant's route must actually stop in the market to earn a local partnership benefit. Recheck on the game's monthly market cadence, but cache the community-side result at a slower pulse if possible.

Player actions: commission a home merchant to bring a needed good using vanilla route/commission mechanics where they permit it; fund a real shipment of grain for crisis relief; arrange paper/books for a sofer or yeshiva; and use a merchant's contacts to carry refugees or ransom news. The installed good `trade_good_books_paper` supports a concrete learning link, but a physical copy in the community library still uses the Sefer acquisition/ownership system, not a generic commodity count. Tie market access to the relevant charter term and ruler protection. Contracts are offered by named characters and have cost, duration, failure, and rival reactions.

### 4.1 A Jewish Quarter district, with the existing Kehillah underneath (Daniel's follow-up, 2026-11-17)

**Recommended design:** make a real `kehillah_quarter_district` in the province where the Quarter domicile sits. The community title, domicile, officers, buildings, treasury, three pillars, and succession remain the only authoritative community state. The province district is its visible **address** and its interface to the host's holding, market, and settlement. Thus a player selecting Worms sees a **Jewish Quarter** tile among the holding's districts, can identify its Kehillah and leader, and can open the familiar Quarter view. Selecting the Quarter still shows the synagogue, yeshiva, countinghouse, hekdesh, mikvah, and sofer workshop with their existing upgrade controls. No second set of construction costs or pillar bonuses is created.

| Layer | What it owns | Player-facing role |
|---|---|---|
| Community title and Quarter domicile | Leader, succession, buildings and levels, funds, pillar variables, books, loans, charter relationship | Playable Kehillah and its existing building/decision UI. |
| Province district | Physical presence in the holding, a required primary building, and at most a small **host-side** local effect | A county-view card, map/holding context, leader and Standing summary, and a route into the community UI. |
| Host charter | Protection, access and financial obligations | Determines whether the district is protected, constrained, or exposed; changing policy does not silently rewrite the title. |

The district definition needs a `primary_building`, but its first version can have **no extra district building list** (`buildings` is optional in `common/districts/_districts.info`). Its primary building is a single fixed **Jewish Quarter** anchor, with no upgrade path competing with the domicile's synagogue. If any county or market modifier is added, it represents a benefit to the *host place* (for example a small local market or settlement effect), not a second grant of the countinghouse's community income. The tile's detail/tooltip should read live community data instead of copying those values to province variables. A later native visual row for facilities may show the domicile's current building names and levels, but must be read-only; upgrades remain in the Quarter. A small holding-view GUI extension may be needed for that row and the **Open Kehillah** action. Neither navigation nor dynamic text is proved by source alone.

**Location and slots.** A regular district would make the Quarter part of an ordinary holding's district budget. That is mechanically interesting: its presence occupies urban space and the host has a concrete reason to negotiate protection, access and tax. It also needs an entry in each relevant holding type's `districts` list (`common/holdings/00_holdings.txt`), so it has compatibility cost with other mods editing that file. Worms's 1066 capital, `b_worms`/province 2731, is a castle holding with province size 4 and existing Walls and Urban Housing regular districts; the installed size-4 modifier gives two added regular slots on top of the base one, apparently leaving one ordinary slot for a Quarter at that bookmark. Other starts and older saves may be full. Do not evict an existing district or permanently inflate settlement size to force the Quarter in. The technical spike must determine whether a regular district can be added by script to an existing holding, whether a reserved slot can be granted safely, and how a host can build or remove it. If regular placement cannot be made reliable, a separately slotted special district is a fallback to test, but existing special sites must not be displaced. The Republic trade-post slot is not used.

Native district construction and effects are scoped to the **province and its barony holder**, so the county ruler may control the visible district even though the Jewish leader controls the community. That asymmetry is useful for charter politics but cannot let the host buy the Kehillah's synagogue or erase its playable title through a routine district replacement. Set `can_be_replaced = no` if the engine permits this for the chosen district type; hide/guard ordinary construction actions that would duplicate the community. A host's closure or seizure must go through the existing policy, expulsion, or crisis story. A new community's founding should create or reserve its visible district only after its Quarter and location exist; pre-authored starts can seed it in history if the port's province history permits. Existing saves need a one-time idempotent reconciliation.

**Lifecycle and multiple communities.** The source of truth is the community's domicile location. Moving or refounding a community moves the district representation; a destroyed community removes or marks the district through the existing place-memory record, without deleting the title first. A damaged Quarter can show a damaged state in the district card, while its actual losses stay in the current crisis/pillar code. Several Kehillot can share a province (the mod's domicile-pin research explicitly allows this), so do not assume one district per title. One local Jewish Quarter district may list all resident Kehillot and open a chooser; it must never silently show one rite as the only community. If a district cannot accommodate this cleanly, keep the existing multi-domicile map pin as the navigation fallback.

**Proof before building:** first script a tiny `kehillah_quarter_district` with a fixed primary building, no extra effects, and a debug event to place/remove it in Worms. Check regular slot behavior, host ownership, title/domicile survival, county-view rendering, and save/reload in a Luna-only live test. Then test a full-slot county, a second community in the same province, host succession, community succession, expulsion, and a 1.20-to-1.21 save. Only after those pass should the district receive market effects or become the default presentation for all communities. This is a concrete proposal, not an assertion that the engine supports every step yet. Paradox's [1.21 modding diary](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1158310/announcements/detail/699905862545705792) confirms regular/special slot types, holding allowlists, required primary buildings and optional district building lists; the installed files above and `game/common/holdings/00_holdings.txt`, `game/common/defines/00_defines.txt`, `game/common/modifiers/00_basic_modifiers.txt`, and `game/history/provinces/k_east_francia.txt` supply the Worms details.

## 5. Other opportunities and guardrails

- **Books and messengers:** merchant routes can make named books, scholars, and letters easier to *discover or move*, while the collection's actual copies remain title-owned. A disrupted route may delay a promised sefer, not delete a book already on a shelf.
- **Diverse households:** a merchant/bookkeeper family from the existing visit flow can introduce a merchant company, but must not automatically become its owner. Commercial leadership style and the countinghouse are natural optional advantages; scholarly and political styles retain viable alternatives.
- **Competing merchants:** a non-Jewish or Jewish rival can seek market access, challenge a home merchant, or support an ordinance for economic reasons. Hostility attaches to concrete competitors and decisions, not an ethnicity-wide merchant faction.
- **DLC fallback:** free 1.21 markets/districts may inform the local economy without Silk & Silver; native Merchant loans, route actions, and the home-company loop are gated behind `has_gdp_dlc_trigger`. Existing community loans continue under their current EP3 dependency.
- **Performance and save safety:** never scan all world merchants or all routes on every community tick; use local market scope, reverse lists, and periodic reconciliation. Version any durable home-link and loan migration data. Existing saves with outstanding `kehillah_loan_*` remain payable and visible throughout transition.

## 6. Build order and proof required

1. **Port and baseline:** let the 1.21 port land; compare xTiger findings and run a separate live boot before changing mechanics. Record which trade/loan APIs work without Silk & Silver and which require it.
2. **Read-only credit/trade adapter:** local market snapshot, loan provenance/exposure, debtor identity, and diagnostics. Add hidden debug events to log market, home link, debt stock, doctrine, policy, and crisis odds. No effects yet.
3. **Home-community loop:** Jewish adventurer/merchant invitation, company transition, succession and relocation cleanup; one real local route benefit and one reciprocal funded action. Check merchant and community perspectives live.
4. **Doctrine/policy and crisis:** display lawful instruments, add lending posture/charter response, then bounded outlook, faction, and debt-opening effects. Probe favorable credit, a large debt, repudiation, and cancellation separately; compare error logs with baseline.
5. **Native loan backend only if proved:** test community task-contract origination into `story_cycle_loan`, repayment, default, multiple creditors, death on both sides, forgiveness, expulsion, and old-save migration. Replace the old ledger only when each case has one authoritative loan and the existing narrative still resolves. Otherwise keep the current contract ledger and ship the other slices independently.
6. **District representation (Daniel's follow-up):** run §4.1's isolated placement/ownership/UI spike before any district-bound market benefit. If regular placement works, add a single Quarter district as the province view of the existing community, with old-save reconciliation and the multi-community case. If it fails, test a non-conflicting special slot; retain the Quarter domicile and existing map pins either way.

Each scripted slice gets xTiger first and a source audit. Engine identity, inheritance, and save migration need a Luna-only live scripted probe under the repository testing rules; visual checks follow only for player-facing rendering. Record each session under `docs/testing/`. No part of this document asserts a live-game pass.
