Crusader Kings III mod · Pre-alpha tester guide

Jewish Communities

Lead a Kehillah, a self-governing Jewish community inside a Christian or Muslim realm. You hold no land. Your seat is the Jewish Quarter of your host's city.

This is a pre-alpha. Most systems work, but many have been tested by one person only, and some not yet in a real campaign. You will find bugs. Saves from one pre-alpha build may not load in the next, so start a new game after each update.

You build the Quarter, appoint a council and a rabbinic court, gather and study books, train and ordain rabbis, and keep the community alive through its host's favour, through crises and through changes of leadership. Three pillars track how it is doing:

ProsperityPopulation, trade, buildings, income
StabilityCohesion, justice, protection
GreatnessLearning, piety, renown

Each pillar runs from Crisis to Legendary. Most of what you do moves one of them.

Before you install

Game versionCrusader Kings III 1.20.x (Crozier)
Required DLCRoads to Power. The Kehillah is built on its landless government, and the start screens are locked without it.
Optional DLCRoyal Court, Legends of the Dead, The Fate of Iberia, By God Alone, All Under Heaven. Without them, the content that uses them is skipped.
Other modsPlay it on its own for now. Mods that change religion, governments or the government UI will probably conflict.
SavesNot guaranteed to carry over between pre-alpha updates.

Install

  1. Open the Workshop link shared on Discord and click Subscribe.
  2. In the Paradox launcher, add Jewish Communities (Pre-Alpha) to a playset with no other mods, and press Play.
  3. Under Play, pick one of the three Kehillah start screens below.

What's in it

Your community

  • A landless government. The leader is styled Parnas, or Rav if ordained.
  • Leaders are appointed, not born. When yours dies, you continue as the community's choice.
  • Scholarly, Political or Commercial leadership styles new
  • Step down and take to the road. A Jewish adventurer can found, visit or claim a community.
  • A year of Stability in Crisis, and the community collapses.

The Jewish Quarter

  • Nine building lines and 25 tiers: Synagogue, Beit Midrash and Yeshiva, Countinghouse, Market Stalls, Workshops, Mikvah, Hekdesh, Sofer's Workshop, Slaughterhouse.
  • Higher tiers need higher pillar bands.

Council and households

  • Shtadlan, Gabbai, Rosh ha-Shmirah, Ne'eman and Dayyan, each with their own tasks.
  • Rosh Yeshiva, Sofer, Shochet, Gabbai Tzedakah and Mikvah Attendant.
  • Householder families who arrive, add to the pillars, and leave when things go badly.

Rabbis, books and yeshivot

  • The Rabbi trait, with Talmud, Torah commentary and Jewish thought tracks.
  • About 170 historical works in the library and the Sefarim Ledger, ranked by your rite.
  • Write your own sefer. Reviews, approbations and the cherem new
  • Yeshiva students, study partners and ordination.

The Bet Din

  • Three judges hear a docket: a captive's ransom, a silversmith's dispute, a contested dowry.
  • Write to a rabbi in another community for a responsum.
  • Regional synods, and the long road to a Sanhedrin.

Your host

  • A charter of rights from the ruler.
  • A Jewish Settlement Policy in every realm, from Banned to Encouraged.
  • Expulsion, with a year to plead, pay or leave.
  • Loans, translations and Hebrew tutoring contracts.
  • The Nagid, a realm-wide office new

Danger and crisis

  • A crisis is a race between two bars, the Armed Host and Spared, with every source of points named.
  • Post a watch, seek protection, answer the agitator, appeal to the emperor, proclaim a fast, or pay.
  • If the host rises, a second race decides whether the quarter is held or struck.
  • Six openings, from a wandering preacher to Granada, 1066.

Faith, culture and family

  • Medieval rabbinic rites and Karaite communities, with their own tenets.
  • Rosh Hashanah, Pesach, Hanukkah and Purim events, and festival feasts.
  • Patronyms, Kohen and Levite lines, Ben David lineage.
  • Jewish map modes and a realm-wide view of communities.

Your first hour

Play as Isaac of Worms and try each of these:

  1. Hover each pillar on the Domain screen and read why it is what it is.
  2. Pick a leadership style.
  3. Fill any empty council seat and give each councillor a task.
  4. Build something in the Jewish Quarter.
  5. Open the library, look through the Sefarim Ledger, and acquire or study a book.
  6. Convene a Bet Din and get through its docket.
  7. Open the Jewish map modes and click another community.
  8. Play until a holiday event fires.
  9. If a crisis opens, play it out, and tell us whether the race made sense.
  10. Play until your leader dies or steps down, and continue as the successor.

Then try another start: Joseph in Granada for crisis play, Rashi for a harder economy, or David ben Hezekiah as an adventurer.

  • David ben Hezekiah's Exilarch claim

Known issues

  • Some text may be missing or show a raw key such as kehillah_something_desc. Send a screenshot.
  • Balance has had only a rough pass.
  • Campaigns past about 50 years have not been played, so late-game performance is unknown.

Reporting a bug

Post in the Discord testing channel with:

  1. What happened, and what you expected.
  2. Which start and character, and roughly the in-game date.
  3. A screenshot, if it's something you can see.
  4. Your error log after a crash or breakage: Documents\Paradox Interactive\Crusader Kings III\logs\error.log. The game rewrites it every launch, so copy it before restarting.
  5. For a crash or a stuck game, a save from just before.

Feedback that isn't a bug is just as useful: what confused you, what felt unfair, which events you liked.
