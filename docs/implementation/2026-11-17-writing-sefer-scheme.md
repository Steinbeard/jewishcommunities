# Writing a sefer as a long project

**Status: BUILT through 2026-11-18; source and xTiger checked, live UI/save check pending.** Implements [the writing scheme specification](../spec/2026-11-17-writing-a-sefer-scheme.md), superseding the survey and no-manuscript decisions in [the earlier flow build](2026-11-17-writing-sefarim-flow.md). The earlier record remains a diagnosis of the duplicate-book bug and PR history.

`Write a Sefer` opens a free discipline event. Each discipline leads to project forms with one shared scripted gate; approachable locked forms remain greyed out. Choosing a form pays Piety and starts a self-directed personal scheme the following day. At least three 320-progress phases follow: source research, an objection, then completion. A costly long revision adds a fourth phase. Phase options alter draft quality; Learning and that quality set the scheme's visible completion chance. A learned Jewish courtier can become a chavrusa, giving a chance bonus based on Learning and language. Study Torah can now ask for a chavrusa too.

Ordinary completion uses the existing artifact and nearby-news effects but registers no ledger work. The artifact name is then set from the selected project form and matches the completion and news text. Landmark completion registers one fixed catalog ID, automatically gives its author mastery, adds it to the author's library and creates an original-manuscript artifact with that same work ID. A failed attempt yields Learning experience but no book. The landmark artifact uses CK3's top `illustrious` rarity plus a distinct manuscript modifier; the ledger's rite-specific reception remains separate.

All ten preexisting named ambitions and 18 additional post-867 catalog works are reachable only as exceptional outcomes of the appropriate form. The Rashi Torah niche additionally needs six mastered Parshanut works, two Tanakh rounds and 65 Parshanut Rabbi XP; the Rashi Bavli niche needs four mastered Bavli orders. Historical identity affects some old ambition routes but never reserves the slot. The two Rashi display titles use the actual writer after a newly written niche is filled and the historical name for a seeded work. A filled niche leaves the generic form available. Yerushalmi commentaries can target an order the author actually mastered; their authored work begins with lower standing, while subsequent citations can improve the source work's standing.

## Period correction — 2026-11-17

The first build accidentally required post-867 books for every dialogue, disputation and devotional path. Machshavah now has early Scripture dialogue, disputation and ethical-exhortation routes based on the Torah or Tanakh plus the Mishnah, Philo or *Memar Marqah*. Systematic dialogue is an additional path dated from *Emunot ve-Deot* (933); inward devotion is an additional path dated from *Chovot ha-Levavot* (1080). Both still require actual mastery of their source. The older forms stay available later, and the two later forms carry the *Kuzari*/*Moreh* and *Sefer Hasidim* landmark chances respectively. Period-specific choice text and form-specific objection text make the distinction visible.

## Outcome and catalog correction — 2026-11-18

The first build incorrectly treated ordinary success as a ledger entry. Ordinary completion now calls the artifact-only book effect and preserves a title tied to its project form through completion and local news. The generated landmark selector maps all 28 fixed works dated after 867 to a form, verifies the current writing gate and work-specific qualifications, then fills at most one unoccupied niche on a high-quality roll. For the 18 formerly automatic fixed works, a player can fill the niche before its catalog date; the library's generated availability tests now accept either that early authored state or the historical circulation date. The ledger composer, year, title and descriptive text follow the actual writer in the early-authored branch and retain their historical defaults otherwise. The ten old named ambitions keep their prior gates. New projects cover prayer orders, chains of tradition, scriptural law and communal chronicles.
