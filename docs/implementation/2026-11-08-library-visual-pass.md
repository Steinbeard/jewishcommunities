# Book shelf, work page and ledger visual pass — 2026-11-08

**Status:** Implemented in the main worktree; source checked and xTiger baseline unchanged. Live rendering and interaction checks pending. The Luna live tester could not establish CK3 process ownership through CIM and had no xTiger game tools, so no game was launched or controlled.

This revises the presentation of [the shelf and ledger build](2026-11-08-library-shelf-and-ledger.md) without changing work ownership, rite standing or the known-work rule.

## Books

- A work's stable catalog position selects a small title cartouche. It remains the same when the binding tier changes with the viewer's rite. In-play authored slots retain a numbered cartouche. The title and standing remain available on hover.
- The seven standing-based bindings gain subtle surface variation. The six Bavli orders have separate labelled folios; the two Tanakh codices have separate labels. Gaps remain visible for missing volumes.
- The shelf viewport is shorter so its ledger route follows the collection more closely. Foundation status uses a compact count.

The cartouches are abbreviations, not full legible titles at shelf scale. They identify recurring shapes and colours while the tooltip supplies the unambiguous name. The assets remain deterministic outputs of `scripts/kehillah_generate_library_ui_assets.py`; catalog-position values are generated by `scripts/kehillah_library_catalog.py`.

**Painted art revision:** The generated DDS strips now crop from three transparent source atlases in `gfx/interface/icons/kehillah_library/`: `kehillah_spine_art_atlas.png` contains twelve leather, vellum and quire bindings; `kehillah_centrepiece_art_atlas.png` and `kehillah_centrepiece_empty_atlas.png` provide the ark and lectern with and without their books. The source images are kept so regeneration does not discard the artwork. The script maps material to the existing seven standing tiers, applies restrained genre tint, and preserves each frame's CK3 dimensions. The six Bavli folios and two Tanakh codices use painted bindings with separate identifying cartouches. Empty gaps and the title mark layer remain unchanged.

**Hebrew cartouches:** The stable marks now use short Hebrew abbreviations keyed to the historical works. Numbered books authored during play use Hebrew numerals, including the conventional forms טו and טז. The Bavli order and Tanakh codex labels use Hebrew too. `frank.ttf` provides the compact lettering; a Unicode fallback and explicit right-to-left handling keep the art reproducible on other Pillow installations. These are compact identifiers rather than full titles; the work tooltip gives the full name.

## Work page

The domicile's attached library and separate Library window are wider. The selected work's standing is a short value with the ladder and ceiling on hover. Its description, provenance and tenets have more width; the actions are grouped with mastery status. Missing works offer Seek a copy directly. A scrollable work body keeps other libraries and readers reachable in the fixed-height window.

## Ledger

Rows show the title, date, author and standing, copy status and acquisition action. Selecting a title opens a compact catalogue card with description, provenance, author and tenets, plus a route to the full work page. Copy filters select all, held, missing or being sought works. Date and name sorting rebuild the same known-work list with the same admission conditions. Historical name order uses generated English titles; authored titles are assigned during play and remain after historical works in slot order.

The catalog explanation is on the subheader tooltip instead of a footer that overflowed the frame. The ledger header and subheader keys already existed in `kehillah_library_shelf_l_english.yml`; a full restart is still required to load this localization and the registered ledger window in an older running game.

It says whether you know the language, with vanilla's `[language|E]` concept link. If you don't know it, it points to vanilla's Learn Language scheme.

## Review pass 5 — 2026-11-10: the tier ladder, banned books, the Talmud bug, other rites

**Status:** Implemented. xTiger reports nothing new (205 reports, same as the baseline). Not checked in the game yet.

Daniel asked for five things: clearer tier tooltips and styling, a way to see other rites' and faiths' tiers and known books, how many books exist at the start, why the Talmuds show as banned, and banned books that look scarier.

- **Why the Talmuds read as forbidden.** The cause was a 1.20 rule: a faith's doctrines reach only its main rite. The same rule is noted in `common/religion/doctrine_types/kehillah_rite_authority_doctrines.txt`.
    - The generated standing values asked `rite_has_doctrine = temple_authority_rabbinic_doctrine`, which is false for Minhag Ashkenaz, Merkabah and any rite founded in play.
    - Those rites gave the Mishnah and both Talmuds standing 0 (Rejected). The library counts standing 1 or below as forbidden, so these books showed as forbidden too.
    - The new rite-scope triggers `kehillah_rite_rabbanite_trigger`, `kehillah_rite_toric_trigger` and `kehillah_rite_samaritan_trigger` (`common/scripted_triggers/kehillah_rite_tradition_triggers.txt`) ask the rite first, then its faith through `rite_faith`.
    - Every Temple Authority check now uses them: the generator's canon, baseline and floor values, the named-work ambition trigger, and `kehillah_is_karaite_character_trigger`.
    - The standings an existing save has already seeded keep their old baselines, which were one step low for those rites. Canon books are computed fresh, so the Talmuds are fixed even in old saves.
- **The tier ladder.** The fourteen tiers fall into six bands, highest first, each with its own colour:
    - Canon, fixed by doctrine (purple);
    - Honoured (gold);
    - Accepted (green);
    - Unproven (grey);
    - Disputed (light red);
    - Forbidden (red).

    Every place that names a standing now prints `KEHILLAH_STANDING_FMT_<n>`, the name in its band's colour: the shelf rows, the panel, the work page, the ledger, the corpus captions and the hover card. Banned is bold red behind a warning icon.
    - Each tier's tooltip (`KEHILLAH_STANDING_TT_<n>`) gives the name, its band, and what the tier means. It then lists the whole ladder band by band with that tier in bold.
    - The Standing column head's tooltip (`KEHILLAH_LIB_LADDER_TT`) ends with the same ladder, with no tier picked out.
    - A scratchpad script wrote all of these keys. They reference `KEHILLAH_STANDING_<n>`, so renaming a tier still updates every place.
- **Forbidden books look forbidden.** A book your rite bans (1) or rejects (0) is drawn differently in four places:
    - **The shelf:** the spine is darkened, bound with two red cords and sealed with vanilla's warning icon. These books only ever use the two narrowest bindings, so the overlays size to the spine.
    - **The hover card:** it now has a Standing line in colour, plus a red warning.
    - **The panel:** it has the same red line under Standing and Language.
    - **The work page:** it has the red line under its Standing row.

    The red lines describe the ban only. The piety and yeshiva-fame costs stay on their modifiers, per the rule that the loc never restates effects.
- **Other rites and faiths in the ledger.** The rite selector already listed every living rite of every Jewish faith. Choosing one now does three things:
    - It also rebuilds the LIST: the works that rite's followers know of, through `kehillah_lib_refresh_rite_catalog_list_effect` / `_name_effect`, generated by `rite_known_lines`. Canon and Foundational or Essential works count once they circulate; any other work counts once a library of that rite's followers holds it, or once the rite respects it (6+) and a copy exists.
    - The subheader and its footnote change to match ("Followers of X know of N works").
    - A faith label follows the rite name, so Karaite and Samaritan rites read as such.

    Order and rite now both go through `kehillah_ledger_rebuild_effect`, with the order kept in `var:kehillah_ledger_by_name`. Opening the ledger resets it to your own rite, by date. The check marks, copies and binding icons stay your own.
    - Non-Jewish religions are not offered: no non-Jewish rite has standings.
- **How many books.** The catalog holds 143 works.
    - 100 are slots for books written in play.
    - The other 43 are historical; 32 of them are written by 1066 and 25 by 867.
    - The ledger lists only the books your community knows of, not those of every Rabbanite rite. Canon and great works are listed everywhere; anything else is listed once your library holds it, you or a courtier has mastered it, or your rite respects it and a copy exists.
