The [Mishnah-audited workbook](../research/bet-din-docket/CK3_Bet_Din_Docket_250_Cases_Mishnah_Audited.xlsx) is the exact file Daniel shared from his ChatGPT Library. Its SHA-256 is `BB099DF4FE3AFE3F5F688F0F69DDBA3220144D3FCB16FE16AA36456BD8C6A1F4`. It contains 250 proposed cases, 1,250 option prototypes, and cross-references to the Mishnah. The workbook's own **Ruling Options** sheet labels every choice `DESIGN PROTOTYPE / NOT HALAKHICALLY APPROVED`; **Research Audit** leaves case-specific medieval application and editorial sign-off open. It is a research source, not executable Paradox script.

The workbook classifies 18 cases as adapted from an identifiable record, 220 as documented problem types, and 12 as plausible syntheses. Its Research Audit has a checked primary document landing page for the 18 adapted cases; 38 other cases have only a checked analogy, and 194 have no case-level document attached. Seventy-eight entries explicitly use a generic regional placeholder. **All 250** are marked as needing expert editorial sign-off. These counts describe the workbook's audit fields, not independent verification of its historical or legal claims.

## First case: BD-170, The Failing Mikveh

This case fills the Tahorot gap identified in [the rulings and gates spec](../spec/2026-11-11-rulings-sermons-and-gates.md). It is drawn only after 1100 in Christian-ruled Iberia, when the community has a mikvah and two suitable litigants. The donor alleges a defect in the bath's water channel; a hidden fact says whether the join is defective. The court may close and rebuild, inspect and repair if needed, or leave the bath open. Skilled judges can cite a mastered Tahorot work or conduct a joint inspection. An actual defect sharply lowers the quality of a leave-open ruling; closing a sound bath imposes a smaller quality cost. The final event reveals the channel's condition, changes the community's pillars, records the ruling, and advances the existing docket.

The workbook's primary link is [Mishnah Mikvaot 6:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Mikvaot.6.1), concerning water joined to a mikvah. The short quotation in the source tooltip identifies the subject. It does not by itself determine whether this fictional channel is valid; the game makes that a separate fact. This hearing adapts the workbook's premise and facts to the existing three-judge sequence. It does not copy the workbook's five generic options or its 0–100 scoring suggestion, because neither is a verified ruling and the mod's current docket uses a different tally.

The mod tracks mastery by whole work and Mishnah order, not by individual tractate. Case 15 therefore accepts a mastered Tahorot work; it does not claim to implement the workbook's proposed `mastery_mishnah_mikvaot` flag. The bench's Tahorot list and citation system use the existing order-wide code. This is an explicit departure from the workbook's proposed tractate bonuses.

## Second case: BD-011, The Brother and His Half-Sisters

Case 16 adapts the [1156 Geniza record Yevr.-Arab. I 1700.29](https://geniza.princeton.edu/en/documents/9637/) for communities in northeastern Africa in 1156–1170. The record says a brother agreed to pay monthly maintenance to his two half-sisters in accordance with their father's will. It does **not** establish the workbook's allegation that estate assets were withheld. The event corrects that premise: the will and the sisters' maintenance claim are known, while the fictional hidden fact is whether the estate accounts omit another obligation. Two randomly selected litigants represent the brother and sisters, so the event makes no claim that those characters are actual siblings. Its three directions are immediate payment, interim support with an audit, or suspending payments until accounting. The first and third incur fact-sensitive quality costs. The court does not purport to determine inheritance shares or a legally binding payment amount from a generic tally.

[Mishnah Bava Batra 8:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Bava_Batra.8.1) is a relevant inheritance starting point, but it does not establish this estate's accounts or the father's exact maintenance direction. The existing Nezikin mastery system supplies a citation path; the workbook's tractate-specific `mastery_bavli_bava_batra` bonus is not implemented. The event uses the same bench, tally, party responses, pillar outcomes, log and docket advance as the other cases.

## Third case: BD-020, The Rented House of the Orphans

Case 17 adapts [Geniza fragment T-S 12.554](https://geniza.princeton.edu/en/documents/7265/). Its date is not preserved; the proposed 1100–1138 window comes from the workbook and the identified scribe's dated career, not from a date on this fragment. The surviving text describes the court securing orphans' rent from an inherited house with a pledged sugar workshop and copies of the agreement. It does not document the workbook's claim that a former partner contested the rent. The event instead presents a proposed lease with a fictional hidden fact: whether the guarantor has clear title to the offered workshop. The court can accept the pledge immediately, verify and record it, or seek a different tenant. An unverified, defective pledge lowers the first ruling's quality; rejecting sound security delays the orphans' rent. Randomly selected litigants represent the guardians and proposed tenant.

The workbook links [Mishnah Bava Metzia 8:8](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Bava_Metzia.8.8), a lease-duration analogy that does not itself decide whether the guarantor owns the security. Case 17 uses the existing Nezikin citation and bench path. Its pillar tradeoff gives immediate rent a Prosperity gain and charges for verification or vacancy; the legal-quality tally separately reflects the hidden security fact.

## Fourth case: BD-221, The Aid Refused to a Stranger

Case 18 uses the workbook's fictional premise without making a specific historical claim. A traveler needs a meal; the collector cannot find the donor's terms before dusk. The court can spend from the disputed fund immediately, feed the traveler from general stores while checking the deed, or preserve the fund and seek voluntary gifts. A hidden fact determines whether the fund is restricted to local households. The first choice risks violating a restriction; the third delays help, especially when the fund could lawfully have paid. The middle choice protects both needs at a modest Prosperity cost. An existing hekdesh building gates the case. It supplies the docket's first Zeraim citation via [Mishnah Peah 8:7](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Peah.8.7), while the donor's actual terms remain a separate fact.

## Authored batch: BD-021, BD-031 and BD-049

Cases 19–21 use the common three-judge flow through [the batch generator](../../scripts/kehillah_generate_docket_batch.py), with committed event and localization files. The generator removes repetitive event scaffolding but keeps each scene, ruling, tooltip, discovered fact, outcome and log in an authored profile. The cases are fictional adaptations of workbook premises, with no claim that the generated outcome reproduces a historical court decision.

- **The Baker's Missing Capital (19, BD-021):** a supplier's debt and incomplete partnership ledger. Immediate payment protects the bakery at a high communal cost but risks charging the wrong partner; an audit delays final allocation; an equal split closes the venture cheaply but may ignore clear receipts. Drawn in northeastern Africa in 1100–1200, with a Nezikin citation.
- **The Taxpayer Between Two Communities (20, BD-031):** a newly arrived household may have paid the same levy before moving. The court can cover the new town's claim now, fund urgent costs while seeking the receipt, or settle for less. A real prior receipt makes the full claim unfair; no receipt makes the cheap settlement a shortfall. Drawn in Iberia in 1335–1360, with a Nezikin citation.
- **The Scholar Who Changed the Prayer Rite (21, BD-049):** the congregation may welcome or oppose a visiting scholar's proposed reading. Funding immediate change risks division; trial services cost time and resources; preserving the inherited reading may discard a welcome reform. The fictional hearing has no regional gate and uses a Zeraim citation.

All three use the same base tally and Prosperity costs (8 for immediate action, 4 for inquiry, 2 for the inexpensive settlement). A hidden fact penalizes immediate action by 6 when its premise fails; the cheap settlement loses 5 total tally when the contrary fact makes it unnecessary. This shared scale is deliberate for a small batch and should be compared in live play before expanding it. The two judges' review and source options still add their normal tally.

## Second authored batch: BD-032, BD-058 and BD-023

The same generator now emits cases 22–24, each with its own authored scene and decisions. **The Synagogue Roof and the Sacred Fund** asks whether a leaking roof may be repaired from money a donor may have restricted to poor relief; a temporary patch and deed search preserve both needs at a cost. **The Child's Maintenance After Divorce** weighs a father's proposed weekly payment against the child's needs and possible hidden income; an interim payment with an accounts review is the middle course. **The Caravan That Returned Empty** pits an agent's account of a failed journey against an investor's suspicion of concealed sales; checking port receipts costs the court time and money. The hidden fact in each case changes the quality of the quick ruling and the cheap final settlement. Their source orders are Moed, Nashim and Nezikin respectively. They use the same numerical tally scale as cases 19–21, pending live balance review.

## Ten-case writing and balance batch: BD-007, 013, 025, 033–035, 037, 040, 041, 045

Cases 25–34 cover a wife's property and a creditor, an uncle's estate account, a loan copied in two languages, a courtier's tax exemption, a parnas election, a schoolmaster's wages, two congregations sharing a synagogue, refugee families, a tanner's runoff, and a rumor against a slaughterer. The authored scenes, choices, evidence reveals, and tooltip prose live in [`kehillah_docket_batch10_data.py`](../../scripts/kehillah_docket_batch10_data.py); the generated CK3 event and localization files are committed. [`kehillah_wire_docket_batch10.py`](../../scripts/kehillah_wire_docket_batch10.py) adds their draw, citation, bench, cleanup and debug hooks idempotently. Its encoding pass preserves the BOM required by the CK3 script files.

Each case puts an immediate ruling against a costly inquiry and a cheap final settlement. The hidden fact makes the first course risky when evidence contradicts its assumption; the third course has a smaller baseline quality penalty and a further cost when the opposite fact makes it unjust or unnecessary. The three options cost 8, 4, and 2 points of Prosperity, except that the election, shared synagogue, courtyard nuisance, and slaughterer cases spend Stability. Those numbers are initial balance values to be adjusted after a live hearing. The source tooltips name a relevant Mishnah passage from the workbook and explicitly say what the passage cannot establish about the case. Mastery opens a citation option; it never resolves the hidden fact by itself. The cases are playable fiction rather than claims to reproduce historical judgments.
